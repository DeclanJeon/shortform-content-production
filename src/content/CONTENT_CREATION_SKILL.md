# Content creation skill: short-form story production

Apply this checklist when creating a narrative short in this pipeline. Preserve the existing rights, QA, and publication gates; a render remains a draft unless all gates pass.

1. Read the story JSON and extract its cast, beats, narration, and intended tone. Keep the story's established events intact. For newly authored schema3.0 plans apply NARRATIVE_DELIVERY_POLICY.md and the full `storyboarding-video` production-board contract: review one continuous conversational story before allocating shots, preserve its exact words, and carry beat→scene→shot→panel coverage with complete camera/audio/VFX/spatial slots. Existing approved schema1.0/schema2.0 plans are not retroactively rewritten.
2. Define a persona and a distinct, locked visual-DNA sheet for every recurring character (including supporting cast) before generating scene art. Default character treatment: chibi. Character DNA must differ in silhouette, face, hair/fur, eye shape/color, palette, clothing, and mannerisms; shared art style is allowed, cloned identities are not. Use each character's own Codex Imagen sheet as a scene reference and record paths/hashes. Reject frames where characters cannot be distinguished at thumbnail size or drift from their sheets. Do not label locally drawn placeholders as generated imagery. If `codex-imagen` is unavailable, mark generation blocked.
3. Build a narration/caption beat map before render. Give every beat its own authored scene asset; never reuse a still, scene-bank/stock footage, or crop/zoom duplicate to cover another beat. For ~50-second Shorts target at least 16 distinct shots, usually estimated1.5–3.0 seconds each (planning estimates1–4seconds). Final holds follow actual TTS/caption boundaries; inspect rhythm and emotional reactions rather than cutting speech to fit an obsolete estimate. Vary action, shot size, angle, subject focus, and purposeful camera motion; camera zoom alone does not make a repeated still fresh. Open with a visual hook in the first second. Maintain the template header with channel name first and title directly beneath; keep it compact and readable.
4. Before final caption styling, use the Flucto adapter on 3–5 publicly accessible, relevant Shorts when references are needed. Verify the collected media's aspect ratio (do not assume a `Shorts` URL is portrait), save source URLs/status and sampled frames, and record observed caption size as a frame-relative estimate, color/contrast, placement, and visual font family/weight. Separate direct observations from inference; an encoded video cannot verify the source font file. Do not copy channel branding, exact layouts, stories, or wording. Exclude landscape candidates from portrait-style benchmarking and disclose when references come from one creator. If Flucto collection fails, record the blocker rather than inventing findings.
5. Automated render jobs use the versioned profile `implementation/worker/adapters/caption_style_v10.json`: nominal 80px bold white captions, dark translucent backplate, balanced wrapping within 920px and at most two lines. Line wrapping must preserve cue timestamps. The render worker must validate and report the applied profile; until an actual render adapter exists, keep the job `needs_review` rather than claiming a successful render.
6. Set Typecast `tempo` to `1.2` for the continuous narration take. Schema3.0 speaks the reviewed master paragraph; cut boundaries must not impose artificial sentence breaks. Do not send unsupported free-form emotion controls. Regenerate audio and word timestamps together; never speed-adjust audio alone while retaining stale caption timings. Record the applied tempo and audio job IDs.
7. Verify dimensions, codecs, full decode, sampled frames, title/channel text, motion, caption/audio alignment, the actual provenance of every visual asset, and scene diversity. Fail QA for repeated image hashes, missing persona/cue mapping, fewer than 16 unique shots in a ~50-second short, or cloned character identities. Keep unresolved voice rights, creative review, and any other required QA as publication blockers.

`implementation/content/last_bread/render.py` is a legacy Pillow/vector renderer; its placeholder cast and repeated visual motifs are not compliant with the character-DNA/no-scene-bank direction. Do not use that draft as proof of generated art or v2 scene quality. New generated character and scene assets must come from the actual `codex-imagen` capability and pass `validate_shot_manifest.py` before delivery.
