# Storytelling with Still Images

## Principle

Stills are the only picture sources; order, hold time, reframing, transitions, and sound carry the story. “No video-generation model” bans generated footage, not assembling stills and audio into an MP4.

## Activate when

Use for new or existing still-image cuts, with or without audio. Use normal creative production for live-action or generated motion. If new stills are requested, use `codex-imagen`, not a video model.

A planning/read-only/no-generation request permits cue text only, not new stills, audio synthesis, file assembly, or publication. An authorized still edit may encode an MP4, but does not authorize posting it. Unseen assets remain proposed; filenames and successful decoding alone do not establish visible content or legibility.

## Workflow

1. **Lock the contract:** asset IDs/order, aspect ratio, runtime, dialogue to preserve, narration language/voice, subtitles, audio tracks/speakers, and exclusions.
2. **Bound the facts:** distinguish source-backed facts and speakers, interpretation, unknowns, and staged details. Keep suspicion separate from findings; do not invent why evidence differs. Keep provenance in notes unless requested on-screen. Never present reconstructed dialogue as a source quote.
3. **Inspect and map each still.** Missing or unclear image: use a generic ID, mark mapping “proposed—not inspected,” and do not describe unseen content. Possible evidence arc: discrepancy → context → act → consequence → testimony → investigation → outcome/reflection; adapt to the material. Give every hold a purpose. Repeat an evidence image at the end only as a meaningful callback.
4. **Write and time:** short lines, one idea each; remove source-meta wording and repetition. Preserve dialogue and approved wording. Assign recorded dialogue to a speaker only after confirming its track; otherwise narrate the fact without inventing a quote. Use one cue row per visual hold; match dialogue to the character image when possible and avoid overlap. Measure clip duration and pauses; mark estimates until audio exists. If a line exceeds its hold, extend/reallocate when runtime is flexible. If fixed, resolve the conflict instead of rushing speech or cutting preserved dialogue.
5. **Mix and assemble:** prioritize speech over BGM; keep narrator tone consistent, use sparse illustrative SFX, measure loudness/true peak. Assemble only approved stills and audio. Crops, pushes, cuts, and dissolves are allowed; generated footage is not. Honor the subtitle choice.
6. **Verify and deliver:** watch/listen when possible. Check facts, image order, speaker/timing, continuity, ending, runtime, dimensions, frame rate, codecs, audio, and subtitles. Inspect actual crops and text at the intended display size; record which still/time interval was checked and any clipped subject, unreadable glyph, or subtitle overlap. Encode/probe is not story or legibility QA; mark uninspected items unverified. For actual files, use `recording-production-history` and report path/checks. For planning only, return script and cue table.

## Cue-table contract

| Start–end | Still ID + observed content or “proposed—not inspected” | Exact speech | Sound / transition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Measured/estimated | Beat and purpose | Text/audio ID; silence | BGM/SFX, cut/dissolve/reframe |

One row per distinct hold; allocate the ending.

## Derived case pattern

The local v7 case is an illustrative production anecdote, not a measured baseline available in this skill or a timing rule. A returning evidence image can be proposed as a callback; choose holds from the current supplied audio and runtime, not that case's numbers.

## Craft evidence and limits

The film-editing principles below are applied to still holds; that adaptation is a production proposal, not a claim that these books tested still-only videos or established ideal hold lengths.

- **Order, detail, emotional juxtaposition (Principle; Workflow 3):** Reisz/Millar, *The Technique of Film Editing*, `E:/Book/06_Book/originals/콘텐츠_제작/md/Technique of Film Editing, Reissue of 2nd Edition (Karel Reisz, GAVIN MILLAR) (z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk).md#L73-L79`, book pp.14–17, READ-PARTIAL. Supports constructing sequences from significant details and changing perceived meaning by juxtaposition/order. It reports historical montage experiments, not a contemporary efficacy measurement. The evidence-arc order and returning-image callback here are optional local proposals, not a source-prescribed formula.
- **Hold duration and placement (Workflow 3–4; cue table):** Pearlman, *Cutting Rhythms*, `E:/Book/06_Book/originals/콘텐츠_제작/md/Cutting Rhythms Shaping the Film Edit (Karen Pearlman) (z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk).md#L149-L155`, book pp.44–47, READ-PARTIAL; 547079 bytes, SHA-256 `e09d86df3f28ef5e4351e16296a0e26528dfc1ca78aff9abc2c4a7962744e49e`. Supports choosing cut point, duration and placement, with felt duration relative to adjacent shots and information/change. No fixed seconds-per-still, words-per-second or proven retention benefit follows. Measuring supplied speech and resolving runtime conflicts is the local delivery constraint.
- **Sound meaning and selective mix (Principle; Workflow 5):** Reisz/Millar, same exact rendition, `#L97`, book p.26, and `#L477-L479`, book pp.216–217, READ-PARTIAL. Supports sound changing image meaning and selective attenuation around significant dialogue; it does not require speech dominance in every music/abstract work. Speech priority here serves an approved speech-led cut; sparse SFX and consistent narrator tone are proposed mix choices, not universal laws. Loudness/true-peak measurement is a technical check; no numeric platform target is asserted.
- **Reisz/Millar rendition identity:** 910909 bytes; SHA-256 `0b371150dea868a263f3de41d28b9e220cad8db9a3b21f6e540ce0655a970a55`. All three citations above address this original, not another title-matching copy.

Permission boundaries, observed/proposed labels, provenance, dialogue preservation, cue fields and inspection requirements are authored operating contracts. They are not psychological findings; probe/encoding success never establishes story effectiveness or readable crops.

## Acceptance

The assembly plan is complete only when every still is identified as inspected or proposed, each hold has a narrative purpose and timing basis, exact speech and sound cues are assigned without invented quotations, and the sequence has an explicit beginning, progression, and ending. For a rendered deliverable, report actual probe/playback checks for runtime, dimensions, audio, subtitles, image order, and fact handling; mark anything not inspected `UNVERIFIED` rather than treating encoding as story QA.
